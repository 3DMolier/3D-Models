About This Model

A single cancer cell with a rough, spiky membrane, prepared as a printable model for biology and medical teaching. The irregular protrusions across the surface are the feature students remember, since healthy cells look far smoother. Printing it gives a lecture something tangible, which helps explain abnormal growth better than slides alone.

TurboSquid's CheckMate specification shaped the file, so it opens with sensible scale and naming. For renders, a semi-transparent membrane shader with a darker nucleus inside gives a convincing microscope look.

Its 2024 release at $31 opened it to cancer awareness campaigns, medical school lessons and research presentations. Combine it with the dividing cell model to show the cycle that drives a tumor's growth. Painted in contrasting matte colors after printing, the spikes stand out for students at the back of a room without showing fingerprints. Patient support groups also use such physical models to make difficult diagnoses easier to discuss.

By Andrey Simonenko, 3D Molier · Published July 2024 · Updated 12 November 2026
