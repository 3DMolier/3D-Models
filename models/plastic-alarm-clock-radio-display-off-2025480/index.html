About This Model

This plastic clock radio sits on a bedside table with its display switched off, ready for a morning scene before the alarm. The rectangular body, speaker grille and row of top buttons give it the look of a budget bedroom electronics item. Because the screen is dark, it works in shots where glowing digits would distract from the actors or the lighting.

TurboSquid certified the file as CheckMate Pro, meaning the geometry, scale and naming passed a strict manual review. The matte plastic shell takes a soft key light well, and a slight sheen on the edges keeps it from looking flat. If a lit display is needed later, the face panel is the natural place to add an emissive material.

It went on sale in 2023 and suits hotel rooms, dorm interiors and quiet domestic sets in film or games. Next to a lamp and a phone charger, the radio makes a nightstand feel genuinely lived in. Architectural visualizers also use small props like this to give a bedroom render a believable scale reference.
