About This Model

A male arm playing rock paper scissors, shaking the fist through the countdown and opening into each of the game's familiar shapes. The rigged Maya animation covers the whole round, so a quick decision game can be staged without keyframing every finger by hand. Rock, paper and scissors are all clear from the front, which is how the game is usually filmed.

The joints allow a tight fist, a flat open palm and the split two-finger pose without fingers intersecting. Skin shading on the palm and knuckles holds up when two hands face each other in a tense close-up. TurboSquid's CheckMate specification shaped the file, which keeps the hand at real size and every bone named in a way animators will understand.

Launched this year, 2026, for $149, the arm is a natural fit for game shows, children's animation and educational clips about probability and chance. Developers can turn it into a mini-game where the player's hand faces a computer opponent, round after round. Two instances facing each other also make a quick comic duel, the way friends settle who washes the dishes.
