About This Model

A Victorian hand-held stereoscope with a hooded eyepiece and a sliding card holder, made for antique shop and parlor scenes. Looking through its paired lenses at a double photograph produced a single image with depth, the virtual reality of the Victorian era. Families once passed such a viewer from hand to hand around a parlor table after dinner.

The model carries CheckMate Pro certification, which stands for clean topology, real-world scale and properly named parts. The wooden handle and the wire rail that holds the photo card are separate, readable shapes even in a wide shot. The hood around the eyepieces is curved to block side light, the detail that makes the device recognizable at a glance.

Available since 2015 at $29, it has reached 10 buyers, mostly set designers and educators covering the history of photography. It belongs on a side table beside a stack of view cards, in a museum case or in a period film interior. A historian narrating early 3D imaging would hold exactly this kind of device.
