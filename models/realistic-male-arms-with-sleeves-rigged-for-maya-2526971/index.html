About This Model

A pair of realistic male arms wearing shirt sleeves, rigged for Maya and ready to perform first-person actions like grabbing, typing or pointing. Sleeves are rolled to the forearm, so the hands and wrists remain visible while the clothing still suggests a character. The rig controls every finger, giving animators full freedom over expressive hand work.

Skin detail on the knuckles, veins and nails holds up when the camera sits right behind the hands in a close view. As part of the medical range, the arms are also useful for showing how wrists and fingers bend during rehabilitation exercises. Their skeleton, proportions and naming follow CheckMate requirements, so a Maya scene opens tidy and ready for keyframes.

Released in 2026 for $149, the arms suit VR experiences, first-person games, tutorial videos and medical animations about hand movement. Interface designers can show touchscreen gestures with a natural hand instead of a cartoon cursor. Physical therapists and educators also find rigged arms useful for illustrating exercises step by step.
