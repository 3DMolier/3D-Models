About This Model

A heavy hydraulic hammer mounted on an articulated excavator arm is the tool demolition crews use to break concrete and rock. This breaker attachment counts 51,516 polygons and 53,299 vertices, with pistons, hose runs and the chisel tip all clearly defined. The boom segments separate at their pins, which helps when staging different working angles.

Eleven maps at 4096 x 4096 provide chipped yellow paint, greasy steel and rubber hydraulic lines. UV shells stay apart, so dust and wear can be painted heavily near the chisel without spreading elsewhere. The model was built to CheckMate specifications, keeping topology and scale consistent with production standards.

Available since 2019, the arm fits demolition videos, mining visualizations and construction equipment training. Machinery dealers use such models to show how a breaker mounts and swings on a carrier. Set it against a crumbling wall with debris, and the scene clearly communicates heavy demolition work.
