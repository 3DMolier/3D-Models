About This Model

A cancer cell caught mid-division, its body pinching into two daughter cells, prepared as a printable teaching model. The spiky surface and the narrowing waist between the halves make the moment of mitosis readable from any angle. As a print model, it gives classrooms a physical object to pass around instead of a flat textbook diagram.

Its construction meets TurboSquid's CheckMate specification, so proportions and naming are sound from the start. In renders, a soft translucent shader with a warm interior glow makes the dividing cell look alive. Printed in two colors, the halves show the split even more clearly to students.

Since 2024 this $31 print model has served oncology education, biology classes and patient information materials. Pair it with the single cancer cell model for a before and after sequence in a lesson about tumor growth. Researchers also use such models to explain why fast division makes tumors aggressive.
