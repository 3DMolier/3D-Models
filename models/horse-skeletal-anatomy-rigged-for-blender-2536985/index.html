About This Model

A complete horse skeleton rigged in Blender, so the bones can walk, rear or stand for veterinary and equine anatomy lessons. Seeing the skeleton in motion explains how a horse carries weight far better than a static plate in a textbook.

The long skull, deep rib cage and slender lower legs show the build of a running animal at a glance. Each joint follows real equine movement, so the knee and hock fold the way riders and vets expect them to. Blender holds the controls natively, which saves converting a rig that relies on many small joints in the legs. Hooves end in solid bone caps that show clearly how the lower leg meets the ground surface.

Introduced in 2026, the skeleton serves riding schools, veterinary colleges and museum style exhibits about horses. Ivory bone shading reads cleanly against dark backgrounds used in educational videos and presentation slides. Pair it with a fleshed horse model, and transitions from skin to bone become a powerful teaching moment.
