About This Model

Blank wooden signs on posts, chains and easels give designers a ready surface for menus, trail markers or shop names. Planks with visible grain, rounded corners and rough edges make each board feel handmade rather than cut by machine. Because the faces are left empty, your own lettering or logo can be laid on and rendered as if painted or burned in.

Weathered pine or dark oak finishes are set in the materials, chosen by whether the sign hangs at a café or a campsite. The bundle has a CheckMate Lite rating from TurboSquid, confirming the files open cleanly and the meshes are sound. A mockup like this saves a branding studio from staging a photo shoot just to show how a logo looks on timber.

Three versions are offered for the sign set, priced $31, $44 and $49, each with its own mix of shapes and stands. Dating from 2023, it suits restaurant presentations, rustic wedding invitations and outdoor wayfinding in parks and farm shops. Game artists also use the boards for quest markers and village notice posts, where a blank plank can carry any text.

Questions About This Model

Can the Mockup Wooden Sign Boards Collection model be used in a commercial project?

Commercial use is included. The Royalty Free license allows the model in paid client work, broadcast, published games and print; only redistributing the model file itself is excluded.

What is the Mockup Wooden Sign Boards Collection model typically used for?

Stated applications are game assets, film & TV VFX and product rendering. Because the asset is finished rather than a base mesh, it also holds up as set dressing in scenes it was not specifically built for.

How heavy is the Mockup Wooden Sign Boards Collection mesh?

7,318 polygons and 7,332 vertices, built as polygonal quads/tris. Our Model Bundles & Sets listings sit at a median of 32,250 polygons, so 7,318 is lighter than the typical one.

How clean is the geometry on the Mockup Wooden Sign Boards Collection model?

Geometry is modelled at true scale with a tidy edge flow, and the scene is split into named objects so partial reuse and material swaps stay simple.
