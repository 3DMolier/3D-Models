About This Model

A cutaway view of a basic power transformer, showing the laminated iron core and the copper primary and secondary windings. The 640,364 polygons and 322,058 vertices are packed into the dense coil turns and the stacked core plates. Seen in section, the parts explain how the magnetic field links one winding to the other.

Instead of image textures, it relies on StemCell materials that keep copper and steel consistent across renderers. Non-overlapping UVs leave room for labels or arrows showing the direction of current flow. Colored coils make it easy to tell the input side from the output side in diagrams.

Since 2023 it has been part of physics lessons, electrical engineering courses, energy explainers and technical training. Animate a pulsing field around the core, and the principle of induction becomes clear at once. It also pairs well with substation scenes when viewers need to look inside the equipment.

By Andrey Simonenko, 3D Molier · Published November 2023 · Updated 19 November 2026

Questions About This Model

Can the Electrical Transformer Basic Construction model be modified after purchase?

Yes. The Royalty Free license allows editing the geometry, retopologising, changing materials and adapting the asset to a project. What it does not allow is reselling or redistributing the model file itself.

Does the Electrical Transformer Basic Construction model include materials and textures?

Material and texture contents are listed per file on the TurboSquid product page. As a StemCell asset it ships with PBR materials that stay consistent across the delivered formats.

Which industries use the Electrical Transformer Basic Construction model?

Hardware, Software Development, Simulation and Advertising are the primary industries on this listing, though the Royalty Free license puts no limit on where the model is actually used.

What does StemCell mean for the Electrical Transformer Basic Construction model?

StemCell covers both geometry and shading: the model is delivered across formats with materials that stay equivalent instead of needing per-engine conversion.
