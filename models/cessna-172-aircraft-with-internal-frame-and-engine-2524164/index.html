About This Model

A Cessna 172 shown with its internal frame and engine exposed, ideal for explaining how a light aircraft is put together. The 788,832 polygons and 800,682 vertices go into the ribs, stringers, spars and the piston engine up front. High wings braced by struts and the tricycle gear make the airframe instantly familiar to pilots.

Its mixed UV layout is paired with a CheckMate-built structure, so the cutaway parts line up at real scale. Bare aluminum framing looks best under clean hangar lighting that throws soft reflections along the tubes. Exposed structure invites slow orbiting camera moves that reveal how loads pass from wing to fuselage.

There are two versions here: the Cessna 172 Aircraft with Internal Frame and Engine and the Cessna Disassembled Airplane Structure with Engine. Both arrived in 2026 under an Editorial Uses Only license, which suits flight schools, aviation journalism and technical documentaries. Maintenance instructors use the cutaway to point out where control cables and fuel lines run inside the fuselage.
