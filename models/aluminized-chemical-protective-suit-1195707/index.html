About This Model

A full-body aluminized suit, from boots to hood with a wide visor, for scenes of furnace work, fuel fires and hazardous spills. The Aluminized Chemical Protective Suit holds 6,652 polygons over 6,785 vertices, light enough for a whole crew in one frame. Broad creases at the knees, elbows and waist read as heavy coated cloth rather than soft cotton.

6 texture maps at 4096 x 4096 suit a mirror-like fabric that shows every seam and scuff under studio lights. Soot, burn marks or a hazard label can be painted onto one sleeve without repeating on the other, since no islands overlap. The mesh cleared CheckMate Pro, which checks topology, scale and file structure before release.

On sale since 2017, the suit has found 22 buyers among industrial safety trainers and steel plant documentary crews. Fire services use it in emergency drills, where the glare of the fabric instantly signals extreme heat. It also stands well as a background figure beside a smelting furnace or a burning fuel tank.

By Andrey Simonenko, 3D Molier · Published August 2017 · Updated 14 November 2026
