Preparing models for AI and LLM training

The fastest growing kind of custom order we take is not a new object at all. It is existing geometry made usable by a machine learning pipeline, and it comes in two shapes.

A dataset prepared for training

A subset of the catalog, or new production to fill its gaps, delivered in the structure a training run needs: consistent formats, one scale convention, objects and materials named to a scheme you specify, and metadata and annotation structure agreed before we start.

Your models fitted to your pipeline

Models you already hold, brought up to the same standard: retopology, polycount targets, format conversion, renamed objects and hierarchies, corrected scale and pivots. The work that usually sits between a licensed asset and a usable one.

If the volume is large enough to be a dataset rather than a commission, it is priced as one - see data licensing.

Who owns the result

This is the single biggest lever on price, so it is worth settling first.

Rights to you

The model is yours. It does not enter our catalog and is not sold to anyone else. Price is the band above.

Rights stay with us

You get the finished model to use; we keep the right to publish it in our catalog afterwards. Price drops to roughly a third of the band above.

The discounted route is not automatic. We only take it when the model is something our catalog would carry anyway, and we say no when it is not - a one-off part for a specific machine is worth nothing to anyone but you, and we will not pretend otherwise.

How an order runs

  • 1. Brief and references. Send drawings, photographs or an existing model, plus your technical requirements: target formats, polycount ceiling, scale, naming, engine.
  • 2. Quote. We come back with a price and the rights option it assumes. If the subject is complex the figure is settled on completion rather than guessed up front.
  • 3. Production. We build to your technical requirements, not to a generic house standard.
  • 4. Revisions. No cap. We take the model to final against your requirements, however many passes that needs.
  • 5. Delivery. Finished model in the agreed formats. Source files on request.

An NDA is signed whenever the project calls for one - just say so with the brief.
