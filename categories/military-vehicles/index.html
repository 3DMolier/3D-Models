About Military Vehicles 3D Models

Tanks carry much of the detail here, especially in the tracks and road wheels. A Soviet-designed main battle tank fitted with explosive reactive armor reaches 1,368,860 polygons, covering every armor block, road wheel, track link and hatch. A gas-turbine tank whose silhouette has barely changed through years of upgrades is built in 479,236 polygons with 13 texture maps. Two Cold War French tanks show cast turrets and long guns, and one of them is rigged. A battle-worn light tank has mud caked along its lower hull, and a modular tracked chassis without a turret is ready for a weapon or sensor module.

Infantry and support vehicles fill out the ground force. An eight-wheeled amphibious armored personnel carrier has a water jet at the stern and a hull shaped to float with a squad inside. A tracked infantry fighting vehicle pairs a large gun with an autocannon in its turret. A wheeled self-propelled howitzer in desert camouflage models the spades and stabilizers that mark the change from driving to firing. A rigged military truck can raise its air defense missile canisters, the pose that defines that system in photographs.

In the air, the section holds a Soviet swing-wing supersonic bomber, a long-range bomber with huge contra-rotating propellers and a small helicopter with external troop benches. War films, documentaries, strategy and simulation games, museum displays and training courses all draw on these models. Several, including the self-propelled howitzers and the long-range bomber, carry StemCell certification with standardized PBR materials. The cash-in-transit trucks, with reinforced rear doors and small armored windows, belong in heist films and city traffic instead.
