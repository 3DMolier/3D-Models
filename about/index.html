Three numbers appear around this studio, and they measure different things. We have produced more than 100,000 models since 2003, including commissioned work that was never ours to publish. About 90,000 are on sale on TurboSquid right now. Who We Are

Founded by Andrey Simonenko, 3D Molier specializes in creating production-ready 3D models across vehicles, aircraft, military equipment, medical visualization, architecture, industrial machinery and more.

Every model in our catalog is built to professional standards - clean topology, correct real-world scale, optimized UV maps and fully textured. The majority of our catalog carries TurboSquid's prestigious CheckMate certification, guaranteeing geometry that meets the highest industry quality standards.

Our models are used by studios and professionals in film production, game development, advertising, architectural visualization, medical education, VR/AR and simulation worldwide.

  • Custom 3D modeling - we build any model to your exact specifications

  • Multiple formats - FBX, OBJ, MAX, C4D, BLEND and more

How the studio got here

The catalog is a consequence of the first business, not the plan we started with.

  • 2003 - founded, as an architectural visualization studio. Andrey Simonenko started 3D Molier International doing visualization work, beginning with local residential interiors.
  • Within a year, the work scaled. From apartments to a Microsoft campus and a Las Vegas casino - projects where the asset list is long and the quality bar does not move.
  • The pivot. On projects that size, finding usable 3D models took longer than the visualization itself. Everything bought needed rescaling, retopologising or rebuilding. Producing models to one internal standard turned out to be the more valuable business, and the studio moved to it.
  • Over 100,000 models produced since. Across vehicles, aircraft, naval, medical and anatomy, animals, characters, architecture, industrial equipment and more - the same standard applied across all of them, because it was built for our own pipeline first.
  • Today the catalog is sold through the marketplaces and indexed on this site, and licensed as a dataset to teams training on 3D.

That history is why the consistency argument on our data licensing page is not a marketing line. The standard exists because we were the first ones who had to live with the alternative.

What Certification Actually Means

Marketplace badges are easy to misread, so here is what ours mean in practice.

CheckMate is TurboSquid's manual review programme. A human reviewer opens the file and verifies the topology, that objects and materials are named rather than left as Object001, that the geometry sits at correct real-world scale, and that there is no stray or hidden geometry inflating the poly count. It is not a self-declared label - a model either passes review or it does not get the badge.

StemCell is a delivery standard rather than a quality audit. A StemCell model is authored once and delivered across several formats with PBR materials designed to read the same way in a game engine and in an offline renderer, which removes the usual shader rebuild after import.

Models without either badge are still built the same way - quad-dominant topology at real-world scale, named objects, sane pivots. The badge reflects whether the listing went through the marketplace's review process, not whether the mesh is sound.

We are not a bystander to these standards. Both were shaped with the studios producing at volume on the platform, and we were one of them - we took part in working out what CheckMate should check and what a StemCell delivery should contain. They describe how we were already building, which is why adopting them cost us nothing.

Certification is a marketplace programme, and marketplace programmes change. Ours does not. Every model is built and reviewed to these standards whether or not a badge is issued for it, because the standard is what makes the catalog usable as a whole - and that was true before there were badges.

Who Has Commissioned Us

Beyond the catalog, commissioned work has come from Google, Microsoft, Delta Air Lines, Capcom, Autodesk and IKEA, alongside film studios and game developers. Most of it is not ours to show, which is one reason the produced total is higher than anything on sale.
